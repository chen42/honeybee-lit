# 06. Evidence findings, synthesis, and claims

## Evidence-findings gate

Before a full synthesis, prepare [the evidence-findings report](../templates/evidence-findings.md). Present an evidence matrix with study/report links, model or population, design, intervention or exposure, outcome, reported finding and uncertainty, appraisal, evidence level, and unresolved fields.

Report what the selected evidence supports, contradicts, or leaves unresolved. Identify:

- Convergent findings and the conditions under which they were observed.
- Explicit null and conflicting results, including outcomes that differ within a study.
- Differences in design, model, assay, dose, timing, sample structure, and outcome definition that affect comparison.
- Overlapping samples, multiple reports, appraisal concerns, and source-access limits.
- Missing screening, extraction, appraisal, or other work affecting the proposed answer.
- Proposed synthesis groupings and any methodological decision requiring confirmation.

Separate observed differences from plausible explanations. Do not diagnose the cause of heterogeneity merely because studies used different species or doses. Distinguish a gap in this corpus from established absence of research.

For a full review, confirm the synthesis scope before drafting. Reuse an already approved scope if the findings do not require a change. Do not ask the user to approve factual findings or remove unwelcome results. A focused question can present these checks directly in its answer instead of creating a report.

## Build an argument from evidence

Organize by the scientific question, competing explanations, mechanisms, or other approved groupings. A list of paper summaries is not a synthesis. Within each section, explain the question, relevant observations, methodological differences, and bounded interpretation.

Use primary studies for specific empirical claims and reviews for broader conceptual context. Keep registry evidence, preprints, published studies, and secondary syntheses distinguishable. Do not count a review and its underlying primary study as two independent observations.

Compare findings on their actual scales, outcomes, and contexts. Report supplied estimates and uncertainty where useful. Do not vote-count statistically significant results, infer equivalence from a nonsignificant test, or rank a candidate by publication count. Descriptive evidence maps may count studies, but those counts must not become a causal score or an estimate of effect.

Qualify association, prediction, perturbation, and mechanism according to design and controls. A randomized or targeted manipulation can support a causal interpretation under relevant conditions, but does not automatically establish specificity, necessity, sufficiency, or a molecular mechanism. Bound species and human translation explicitly.

For proposed mechanistic chains, cite each empirical link, name its model and outcome, and mark inferred or unsupported steps. Shared pathway membership and an apparent network connection alone do not establish a causal chain.

## Claim-to-evidence map

Use [the claim-evidence template](../templates/claim-evidence-map.md) for full reviews or central contested claims. Each claim records:

- Exact wording, scientific scope, and whether it is empirical, interpretive, or proposed.
- Supporting and conflicting evidence identifiers.
- Exact source passages, table/figure locations, source hashes, and inspected access level.
- Study relationships, appraisal qualifications, and inferential steps.
- Separate results for bibliographic identity, claim support, and publication-status checks.
- Open queries, verification state, and location in the synthesis.

An empirical citation needs verified metadata and an inspected passage that supports the specific statement. Use adjacent identifiers or the project's citation system. Use PMID links when appropriate to a PubMed corpus, verified DOI links for suitable sources, and other authoritative identifiers when no PMID exists. Never invent or borrow an identifier from a similar title.

A source that is merely topically related does not support a claim. Full-text access establishes availability, not that the relevant methods, result, or limitation was inspected. Abstract-only evidence cannot support details absent from the abstract.

A synthesis may explain why excluded studies are outside scope, but must not use them as unacknowledged supporting evidence. Reclassify them explicitly if the approved question changes.

## Scientific prose

Write for scientists who may not know the subfield. Use neutral, direct language, consistent terminology, and focused paragraphs with clear logical connections. Preserve established acronyms unless an acronym audit is requested.

For prose-heavy deliverables, read the relevant guidance in `weaver-text`'s "Scientific prose" module before drafting or revising long synthesis text: honeybee's source-linked constraints (findings-to-source linkage, coverage statement, records, and approval-scope boundaries) remain in force; the module's craft rules do not replace them. Do not import its manuscript or submission instructions for a short focused answer.

Use past tense for specific completed experimental actions and findings, present tense for established facts and broader interpretations, and conditional or future language for proposed work. Separate observation from interpretation and planned work from completed evidence.

Avoid promotional significance claims, generic future-prospects paragraphs, vague attribution, manufactured punchlines, and formulaic transitions. Use straight quotation marks. Do not use rhetorical em dashes or en dashes in editable prose. Preserve source quotations, titles, identifiers, numerical notation, and code unchanged.

Place specific limitations next to the claims they qualify, with a concise overall coverage statement. End on the most defensible answer and unresolved scientific question, not a generic positive conclusion. Use `[REVIEW QUERY: ...]` for missing information rather than filling gaps.

For requested manuscript or grant integration, read the applicable `weaver-text` guidance and preserve its scope and author-approval boundaries. A request for a literature answer alone does not authorize source-document edits or a submission package.
